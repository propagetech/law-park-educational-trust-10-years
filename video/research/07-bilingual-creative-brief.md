# 07 · Bilingual Creative Brief

For the Law Park Educational Trust 10-year anniversary welcome film.
English and Kannada, one visual master, two narrations.

---

## 1. Objective

Open the anniversary event with a five-minute film that makes a room of children, parents, donors, volunteers, teachers and partners understand what ten years of Law Park Educational Trust actually looks like, and leave them wanting to carry it into the next ten.

The event programme already reserves this slot: *"About Law Park Educational Trust, 5 to 7 min, short video or talk covering the 10-year journey"* ([EventPlanPage.tsx](../../website/components/pages/EventPlanPage.tsx), claim EVT-01). The film must therefore work as a **welcome**, not as a fundraising pitch. The fundraising asks come later in the programme.

Secondary uses, in priority order: the anniversary website, YouTube, WhatsApp and Instagram (short cuts), donor communication, and future presentations.

---

## 2. Audience

| Segment | In the room? | What they need from the film |
|---|---|---|
| Children supported by LPET, many visiting Bengaluru for the first time | Yes, seated at the front | To see themselves treated with dignity, not as a cause. To feel proud, not grateful. Kannada matters most here. |
| Parents and guardians | Yes | Reassurance that the trust that took their child's fee is serious, careful and permanent. |
| Trustees, team, volunteers | Yes | To see ten years of their own work held together in one shape. |
| Donors, Indian and overseas | Yes and online | Evidence. Specificity. Where the money went, verifiably. |
| Partner NGOs, schools, government department | Yes | To be named correctly and thanked properly. |
| Chief guest and any press | Yes | A clear, credible, three-minute understanding of an organisation they may not know. |
| General public, later | Online | A reason to care and one obvious next step. |

The Kannada version is not a translation for a minority of the room. **For the children, the parents, the rural school staff and much of the local press, Kannada is the primary version.** It should be written and performed to that standard.

---

## 3. Primary emotion

**Quiet pride, earned over time.**

Not pity. Not triumph. The feeling of a long job done steadily by people who kept showing up. The magazine already found this register in one line: *"Appreciation for the value of routine, ordinary, mundane rhythms of doing work each day each week with care."* That is the emotional target.

Secondary emotions, in order: gratitude, recognition, invitation.

---

## 4. Core message

> For ten years, Law Park Educational Trust has gone to the child rather than waiting for the child to come to it, and has made sure that a school fee never decides a child's future.

Everything in the film should be traceable to that sentence.

---

## 5. Three audience takeaways

1. **They go there.** LPET travels to villages, sits with families, interviews every child and parent in person, and pays the school directly. This is a method, not a gesture.
2. **It has been ten years, and it is documented.** From one scholarship in Chickaballapur in 2016 to 300 school bags in H.D. Kote in 2025, with libraries, career counselling and pandemic relief in between.
3. **There is a way in.** Nominate a child, volunteer, or partner. The film ends with a door, not a donation box.

---

## 6. Tone of voice, English

Clear, warm, formal Indian English. Short declarative sentences that a non-professional narrator can read without stumbling.

- **Do:** name places and numbers plainly. "Chickaballapur, 2016. One scholarship."
- **Do:** let silence carry the images. The script is deliberately under-written so the edit can breathe.
- **Do:** use the founder's own words where they exist. They are better than anything written for her.
- **Don't:** use "beneficiaries", "underprivileged", "empower", "transform lives", "make a difference", "give back", "bleak futures".
- **Don't:** use superlatives the evidence cannot carry. No "thousands", no "countless", no "across the nation" while every location is in Karnataka.
- **Don't:** describe a child as needy, poor, or helpless. Describe the circumstance, never the child.

Reference register: a well-made institutional documentary, not an advertisement. Think of a considered annual-report film.

---

## 7. Tone of voice, Kannada

Formal, warm, spoken Kannada suitable for an NGO anniversary stage. Grantha-free where possible, but not colloquial.

**Register:** the Kannada of a respected school headmaster addressing a hall, or of a well-written Udayavani feature. Standard written Kannada, delivered warmly. Not the heightened literary Kannada of a stage speech, and not the casual Kannada of a vlog.

**Principles**
- Short sentences. Kannada narration runs slower than English; a 20-word English sentence becomes an unspeakable Kannada one. Target 8 to 14 words per breath group.
- Use active verbs and concrete nouns. Avoid stacked Sanskritic compounds.
- Use ನಾವು (we) rather than the passive. The trust speaking in its own voice sounds honest; the passive sounds bureaucratic.
- Address the audience as ನೀವು with plural respect, never singular.
- Keep emotion in the facts, not in the adjectives. Kannada narration turns melodramatic very easily. Resist ಹೃದಯಸ್ಪರ್ಶಿ, ಕಣ್ಣೀರು, ಅಸಹಾಯಕ.

## 8. Brand and motion direction

Extracted from the repository, not invented.

**Palette** (from [tailwind.config.js](../../website/tailwind.config.js) and the magazine)

| Role | Hex | Use in film |
|---|---|---|
| Navy, primary | `#1c1c2e` | Title cards, lower-third bars, letterbox, end card |
| Navy mid | `#2d2d44` | Secondary panels, gradient partner |
| Gold, accent | `#c9903e` | Rules, year numerals, underlines, the single accent colour |
| Gold light | `#e0b06a` | Kannada and English body text on navy, better contrast than full gold |
| Cream, ground | `#faf8f3` | Light-mode cards, statistics panels, gratitude cards |
| White | `#ffffff` | Primary text on navy |

Do **not** use `#15803d`, the green in `site.webmanifest`. It is a pre-rebrand leftover.

**Contrast check.** `#e0b06a` on `#1c1c2e` gives roughly 8.2:1, comfortably AA and AAA for large text. `#c9903e` on `#1c1c2e` gives roughly 5.9:1, AA for large text only. **Use gold-light for any text, reserve full gold for rules, numerals over 60 px, and graphic elements.** Never put gold text on cream.

**Logo treatment.** The mark is a stylised pair of hands supporting a tree canopy, with a graduation cap over an open book at its centre, in olive-green and warm brown. Every file in the repository sits on a solid black or purple field. Request a transparent original. Until then, place the logo only on a navy or black card where the boundary is invisible.

**Motion language**
- Slow. Everything one notch slower than instinct. Push-ins of 3 to 5 percent over 5 to 7 seconds, never a fast Ken Burns.
- Cross-dissolves of 16 to 20 frames between photographs within a year. Hard cuts only between acts.
- Year numerals in Playfair Display, gold, large, entering from a 20 px rise with a 400 ms ease-out. They are the film's spine.
- A single 3 px gold rule as the recurring motif, drawn left to right over 600 ms. It already exists on the website and in the magazine; reuse it rather than inventing a new device.
- No swipes, no whip pans, no particle effects, no light leaks, no zoom-blur transitions.

**Photography treatment**
- Grade warm and consistent. Most source photographs are phone images from harsh Karnataka daylight; lift shadows, hold highlights, and unify white balance across the decade so 2016 and 2025 belong to the same film.
- Do not convert to black and white to signal "the past". The 2016 to 2019 photographs are already visually distinct; a duotone treatment would read as a stylistic tic.
- Letterbox the sub-HD material inside a cream or navy card rather than blowing it up.

**Layout principles** (carried from the site and magazine)
- Left-aligned text, generous left margin, never centred body copy.
- Small-caps gold overline, then a serif headline, then a gold rule. This is the site's established hierarchy; the film should look like the same organisation.
- Text occupies the lower-left third; the photograph breathes top-right.

## 9. Bilingual typography

**The problem.** Playfair Display, Quicksand and Inter, the three typefaces used across the site and magazine, **contain no Kannada glyphs**. If the Kannada titles are set without an explicit Kannada font, the render will fall back to a system font, or to tofu boxes, and the two language versions will not look like the same film.

**The recommendation**

| Role | English | Kannada | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display, year numerals, main titles | Playfair Display, 700 to 900 | **Noto Serif Kannada**, 700 | Both are high-contrast seriffed faces. The pairing holds. |
| Body, lower thirds, subtitles, statistics labels | Quicksand, 500 to 600 | **Noto Sans Kannada**, 500 to 600 | Quicksand is geometric and soft; Noto Sans Kannada at Medium sits well beside it. |
| Fallback if a single family is preferred across both languages | **Noto Sans** + **Noto Sans Kannada** | same | Simplest and safest. Loses some of the site's editorial character but guarantees consistency. |

Both Noto Kannada families are released under the SIL Open Font License, are free for commercial and broadcast use, and are the de facto standard for Kannada digital typesetting. Alternatives if the production house prefers: **Baloo Tamma 2** (friendly, heavier, good for large title cards, OFL) or **Anek Kannada** (variable, modern, OFL).

**Kannada legibility rules for video**
- Minimum size **1.35x** the equivalent English size. Kannada glyphs carry more internal detail; at parity they mush on compression.
- Minimum 44 px cap height on a 1080p master for any Kannada text meant to be read.
- Line height 1.5 to 1.6, against 1.3 to 1.4 for English. Kannada has tall ascenders and deep vowel signs that collide at tight leading.
- Do not letterspace Kannada. Tracking breaks conjunct clusters visually.
- Do not set Kannada in all caps; the script has no case, and forcing it produces nothing but it does break some renderers.
- Avoid ultra-light and ultra-bold weights. Kannada conjuncts lose their counters at both extremes.
- Give Kannada cards **1.3x the dwell time** of the equivalent English card. This is budgeted in the edit plan in `10`.

## 10. Accessibility requirements

- **Contrast:** all onscreen text at 4.5:1 minimum, 7:1 for anything under 30 px. Use the gold-light on navy pairing, not full gold.
- **Captions:** burned-in optional, but a separate `.srt` is mandatory for both languages. Four caption files total: English audio with English captions, English audio with Kannada captions, Kannada audio with Kannada captions, Kannada audio with English captions.
- **Safe area:** all text inside 90 percent title-safe. YouTube, WhatsApp and event projectors all crop differently.
- **No information carried by colour alone.** Year cards carry the numeral, not just a colour change.
- **No flashing.** No strobe, no rapid cuts under 4 frames. Some children in the room may be photosensitive.
- **Audio:** narration peaks at -3 dBTP, loudness -16 LUFS integrated for online, -23 LUFS for broadcast if required. Music bed at least 12 dB below narration.
- **Legibility for a hall:** assume the back row is 25 metres from a 4-metre screen. Nothing smaller than 40 px on a 1080p master.

## 11. Music direction

There is no audio in the repository. Everything must be licensed or commissioned.

**Direction:** solo instrument, sparse, acoustic. A single sustaining line rather than a chord bed. Something with an Indian tonal centre but no rhythmic drive.

- **Preferred:** solo bansuri or a lightly bowed sarangi over a low, near-static drone, with a piano or nylon guitar entering at the gratitude section.
- **Acceptable:** solo piano with restrained strings.
- **Avoid:** orchestral swells at the "impact numbers" beat, four-to-the-floor percussion, corporate-inspirational piano-and-clap library music, and any track with a vocal, in any language, which will fight the narration.
- **Structure:** enter under the opening at low level, drop almost to silence under the origin story so the first spoken line lands, build gently through the timeline, resolve warm and unhurried under the gratitude and closing sections.
- **The same music bed serves both language versions.** Only the narration stem changes.

If budget allows, commissioning a local Karnataka musician for 5 minutes of original bansuri is both cheaper than premium library licensing and a better story.

## 12. Words, visual tropes and claims to avoid

**Words**
beneficiaries · underprivileged · poor children · needy · helpless · slum · backward · destitute · charity case · saving · rescuing · lifting out of · empowering · transforming lives · making a difference · giving back · bleak · hopeless · countless · thousands · nationwide · pan-India

**Visual tropes**
- Slow push-in on a single crying or unsmiling child's face.
- A hand reaching down into frame to give something to a hand reaching up. LPET's own photographs contain several "reaching for supplies" frames; use the wide, not the tight, and never with the giver's face in shadow above.
- Desaturated "before", saturated "after".
- Children arranged and posed holding a cheque, a banner, or a donor's logo.
- A donor's face larger in frame than a child's.
- Counting numbers spinning up on screen over a child's face.
- Aerial drone shots of poverty.

**Claims that must not appear**
- Any cumulative student, village, district or school total, until Q1 in `06` is answered.
- "Across India", while every named location is in Karnataka, unless the trustees confirm otherwise.
- Any named Union Minister in connection with the award, unless the trustees confirm and evidence it.
- The names Meena, Raju or Sunita, or any child's name at all.
- Any child's health status, bereavement, family violence or disability, in narration or in caption, alongside an identifiable face.
- "Rural Education Foundation", "Children Welfare Society", "Education for All Initiative".
- The Section 80G claim, until the certificate is produced.
- The UPI ID or the payment QR code on screen.
- Any of the 20 unsourced supporter quotes.
